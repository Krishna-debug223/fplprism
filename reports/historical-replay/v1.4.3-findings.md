# v1.4.3 goalkeeper scoring and playing-time check

The model now estimates goalkeeper save points from the chance of reaching **three, six, nine, ... saves in a match**, rather than dividing expected saves by three. FPL awards one point for each complete group of three saves ([official scoring rules](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/2174909/)). The calculation weights starts and cameos separately, including the chance of no appearance. A player with zero current-season minutes but a known previous-season starting role is also exempt from the unobserved-reserve cameo penalty.

Forecasts were generated and hashed before outcomes were loaded in a separate scoring pass. Each double Gameweek contributes one summed player result. The [replay method](README.md) describes the available inputs and caveats.

The exact-rule save estimate makes a small, consistent improvement in average error and within-two accuracy, while slightly worsening RMSE. Goalkeeper-only active within-two accuracy rises from 65.8% to 68.8% in 2025–26 and 63.3% to 65.0% in the current-season sample; goalkeeper RMSE worsens from 2.944 to 3.019 and 3.323 to 3.413 respectively. The correction should not be described as a broad accuracy breakthrough. Top-ten projected-player average actual points were essentially unchanged: 4.783 → 4.789 in 2025–26 and 4.233 → 4.233 in 2026–27 GW4–6. All-player appearance Brier scores are unchanged because this save correction does not alter playing time.

**The active-only within-two result still trails a flat two-point guess.** This is partly a different prediction task: unconditional xPts must include the possibility of no appearance, whereas the active cohort is selected after minutes are known. The current model also misses some hauls. Do not advertise active within-two as beating a simple baseline.

¹ Earlier seasons had different FPL scoring rules, especially defensive contributions. The replay uses today's scoring model against those historical outcomes, so they are robustness checks rather than clean estimates of live accuracy. The 2025–26 season was examined during development, and 2026–27 has only three archived Gameweeks. Historical roster and fixture metadata are reconstructed after matches; exact predeadline injuries, betting markets, previous-season player profiles and fixture-difficulty snapshots are unavailable. This replay cannot measure the protected returning-player case, which is covered by a synthetic regression check.

The next worthwhile improvement is a genuinely predeadline archive of availability, lineups, prices and full fixture context, followed by a new untouched season-level holdout. Further coefficient tuning on the same archive would risk overfitting. The separate calibration research tried position-level linear corrections and a most-likely-integer score; none replaced xPts because they did not improve all-player accuracy, active accuracy and top-pick outcomes together.
